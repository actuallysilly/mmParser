How shift capture works, and exactly what it does

Every piece that turns your Infloww screen, live or recorded, into whole conversations on disk: how a frame is read, how hundreds of screenfuls become one chat, why the 8-hour shift came out as scraps and how that was fixed, and how recordings of Discord streams are handled.

01The big picture

There are two ways chats get in, live and from video. Both produce the same thing, and two tools show it:

Live shift recorder

Watches the Infloww window while you work and records each screenful the moment it changes. chatreader.pyw + shiftlog.py

Video to chat

Does the same to a screen recording (OBS, or a recorded Discord stream), frame by frame. video2chat.py

Shift Viewer

Shows everything collected as a read-only copy of Infloww, so you can check it. tools/shiftview

Infloww clone

The one shared, pixel-faithful renderer of Infloww that the viewer and TestFlow both draw with. tools/inflowwclone

SOURCEThe Infloww window (live) or a video file
↓ one frame
READtranscript.py finds the message pane and reads every bubble, its footer (time, chatter, price, paid), the fan's name in the header and the fan panel on the right
↓ one "read" = one screenful
JOURNALshiftlog.record() appends it as one line to userdata/shiftlog/reads/<date>.jsonl. This raw journal is the source of truth.
↓ merge

↓
VIEW / TRAINThe Shift Viewer shows the chats. Later, training datasets get built from them.

A "read" is the unit of everything here: one screenful of one chat, read from one frame, with a timestamp. A long conversation is hundreds of overlapping reads. The hard part isn't reading the screen; it's stitching the reads back together.

02Ground rules

  • Pixels only. Nothing reads Infloww's internals: no page code, no dev tools, no network traffic. Everything comes from what is drawn on screen, plus typelog for what you typed.
  • Never steals focus. Live capture uses PrintWindow, which copies the window's image even when it's behind other windows, in about 40 ms, without touching the keyboard or mouse.
  • Local only. Chat data stays on this PC. Nothing is uploaded, and the viewer serves its pages only to 127.0.0.1.
  • The window title decides the account. "Infloww Messages - 333" files under account 333. Tools that show chats must therefore never have "Infloww Messages" in their own title, or the reader would read them. TestFlow is the one deliberate exception: it's meant to be read, as a test window.
  • Raw first, derived second. The journal is only ever appended to. Every improvement in this guide was applied to old shifts by rebuilding from it, so nothing already recorded is lost when the stitching gets smarter.

03Live: the chat reader loop

src/services/llm/chatreader.pyw runs in the background (MMA starts it; Settings ▸ Features ▸ "Chat reader"). It also feeds reply suggestions through userdata/chat_now.json; the shift recorder is a passenger on the same loop.

Every 0.7 seconds (POLL_S)

  1. Capture the Infloww window with PrintWindow.
  2. Make three tiny grey thumbnails (96×32 px): the message pane, the fan panel on the right, and the header with the fan's name.
  3. Compare each with the previous poll. A difference above CHANGED = 0.6 counts as "moving".

The settle rule

Nothing is read while anything moves. All three thumbnails must hold still for SETTLE_POLLS = 2 polls in a row. Then everything is read from that one frame.

Why all three, from one frame: when you click another fan, Infloww updates the right panel first, then the messages, then the header name last. On the first live night the reader sometimes read the new chat under the old name, or the old fan's panel under the new name. Reads from 02:04–02:40 that night are partly mislabelled for this reason. Waiting until all three regions are still, then reading them together, fixed it.

What gets read once settled

  • The chat: when the pane or the header changed since the last chat read. Messages, footers, fan name.
  • The panel: when the fan is different from the last panel read, or the panel thumbnail changed.

04Reading one frame

src/services/llm/transcript.py turns one image into a list of messages ("turns").

1 · Find the message pane

Infloww draws its column dividers in exactly #444444. find_layout() looks for two vertical lines of that colour (±2) covering more than 95% of the middle of the window, then two horizontal lines between them: the line under the conversation header and the composer's top line. The rectangle between them is the message pane. The scale comes from the header line's height relative to its reference position (y = 133 on the 1920×1032 reference window). Your live window is 3442×1379 at scale 1.37.

If the dividers aren't where a conversation puts them (the inbox, a settings page, a half-drawn frame), the answer is "no layout", never a guess.

2 · Find the bubbles

Fan bubbles are #262626 on the left; yours are #353535 on the right. Each bubble is cut out and its text OCR'd. Live uses RapidOCR 1.2.3 (PP-OCRv3); on clean live captures a bake-off against newer engines was a draw. Media (photos, videos, PPVs) become text-less turns, and watermark text inside them is dropped by the _MEDIA_JUNK patterns.

3 · Footers ShiftTimes

Under a bubble (4–32 px below, times the scale) sits its footer, for example $15.00 not paid 9:19 pm or 1:03 am ✓ tydrera. parse_footer() splits it into time, date, sender (which chatter sent it), price, paid, and liked. Colour backs up the text:

ThingColourMeaning
Price pill#ff7c71PPV not paid
Price pill#2bd4acPPV paid (from BallLore's notes, not yet measured live)
Heart#3467ffLiked
Media framemeasured per bubbleppv_border: opened / not opened

A footer with no bubble above it (a PPV's image without a caption) is attached to the media turn above it.

4 · The fan's name

The header shows "your nickname for him(his name)" in white, followed by a grey edit pencil. Only near-white ink is kept (NAME_INK: 200 live, 140 for video, where compression dims thin white strokes), so the pencil never turns into extra letters.

5 · The panel ShiftPanel

read_panel() OCRs the right column into its tab (insights / sales / notes), raw rows and named fields: Total spend, Last spend, Spending power, Status, Nickname, and so on. _tidy_value() fixes the OCR's usual slips: $o→$0, $33795→$337.95, $5714k→$57.14k, so Total→$0 Total, PPV I→PPV |.

05The journal

Every read becomes one JSON line in userdata/shiftlog/reads/:

{"at": 1792627441.79,                         ← when (unix time)
 "title": "Infloww Messages - 333",           ← which account
 "fan": "…",                                  ← name from the header
 "resumed": true,                             ← (video only) first read after Infloww was off screen
 "turns": [{"who": "fan", "text": "…", "time": "1:45 am", "price": "15.00",
            "paid": false, "footer": "$15.00 not paid 9:19 pm", …},
           {"who": "model", "text": "", "media_img": "333/<fan>/<hash>.jpg", …}, …]}
{"kind": "panel", "at": …, "fan": "…", "panel": {"tab": "insights", "rows": […], "fields": {…}}}
  • Live reads go to <date>.jsonl; a video goes to <date>.video-<file name>.jsonl. Re-running a video replaces its file, never doubles it.
  • rebuild() deletes chats/ and replays every journal file in name order through the same merge, with name folding (§08). It takes seconds, and it's how today's fixes reached old shifts.
  • Saving a chat file writes a temp file and swaps it in. If Windows says "access denied" because something has the file open for a moment, it retries 20× over 2 seconds.

06Stitching reads into one chat

Each new read of a fan is merged into what is saved for that fan (shiftlog.merge()). OCR isn't perfectly repeatable: the same message can come back as "ur" in one read and "u r" in the next, or with and without its reply-quote header. So matching uses same(), a fuzzy comparison, never exact equality.

Step 1 · Exact fit

First the strict merge from the Live Trainer: slide the read over the end of the saved chat and accept the offset where every overlapping message matches. This handles the common case, including a one-message overlap after a quick scroll.

Step 2 · Diff-style fit

If that fails, align the two like a diff (longest common subsequence under same()). Text matches weigh 2 and media matches 1, so two photos can never outvote a real sentence. The read is accepted when:

  • at least MIN_ANCHORS = 2 text messages match (or all of them, if the read has fewer), and
  • matches cover at least MIN_AGREE = 60% of the read's text messages between its first and last match.

Then:

  • Fill-ins: where the read has messages between two matches that sit next to each other in the saved chat, they are inserted (one read caught something an earlier one missed).
  • Older: messages above the first match are added on top, but only if the match is at the very top of the saved chat (at most 2 media items above it, LOOSE_EDGE).
  • Newer: messages below the last match are added at the bottom, under the same condition at the bottom. They are stamped with the read's time.
  • Matched messages are combined: the longer text wins, missing fields get filled in, and paid / liked / PPV border take the latest value (a PPV turns paid).

Step 3 · It fits nowhere

See the next section; this is where the 8-hour-shift problem lived.

Islands

When you scroll far back, each read overlaps the previous read but not the saved chat. Those reads grow an island of their own, merged with the same rules. After every successful merge, the 8 newest islands (RETRY_ISLANDS) are tried against the chat again. Once a read bridges the gap, the island joins. The viewer shows unjoined islands as orange "Fragment" sections.

One special case: if the saved chat has no text at all (the first read caught the chat mid-load, one empty media bubble), it's thrown away, because nothing could ever line up with it.

07Gaps: the 8-hour-shift bug fixed today

What you saw

After a 13-hour live capture, the viewer showed mostly the latest few messages per fan, not whole conversations.

What was actually wrong

The reader had seen the shift: 723 of the 774 messages you sent (93%, checked against typelog) were in the journal. The stitching lost them:

  1. Infloww always opens a chat at the bottom.
  2. You leave a fan. While you're elsewhere, more arrives than one screen holds, or you send a PPV whose image fills most of the pane (a screen can drop to 3–4 messages).
  3. You come back. Nothing on screen overlaps what was saved, so the merge fails.
  4. The old rule assumed a failed merge meant you scrolled back, and parked the read as an island. Every later read overlapped that island, not the chat, so the whole rest of the conversation grew in the island.
  5. An island only joins when a read bridges the gap, which takes scrolling back, which nobody does mid-shift. So each chat froze at its first gap.
  6. The viewer then put the fragments first and the frozen main chat last, labelled "Recorded chat". The oldest part appeared at the bottom, where the newest belongs.

Before the fix, 848 of 2,599 messages on account 333 were stuck in islands. One fan with 89 reads had 42 messages in his chat and the last 8 hours spread over five fragments.

The rule now: is it newer or older?

When a read fits nowhere, _is_newer() decides:

  1. If any message in the read carries a date, it's an older day: an island, as before. Infloww shows dates only for days before today.
  2. If both sides have footer times: take the last timed message of the saved chat (h) and the first timed message of the read (r). The read is newer when (r − h) mod 1440 < 720 minutes. The mod makes 11:58 pm → 12:03 am count as 5 minutes later, not 23 hours earlier.
  3. With no times, it's newer only if the read is fresh: the first read after switching to this fan (the previous read was of another fan), or, from video, the first read after Infloww was off screen (resumed). A freshly opened chat is at the bottom, so it's the newest part.

A newer read is appended below the chat, and its first message gets gap_before: true. The viewer draws that as a blue line: ··· not seen: messages came in while you were away · back at 4:46 am ···. An older read still becomes an island, which is right for a real scroll-back.

After rebuilding the same journal (account 333):

BeforeAfter
Messages in the joined chat1,7512,383
Messages stuck in fragments848212

Still true: the median fan is about 12 messages because that's what was on screen while you had him open. Anything before the shift is captured only if you scroll up in that chat. To get long full histories of your regulars, scroll up through each one once during a shift; the scroll-back is recorded and joined.

08One fan, many spellings

The header name is OCR'd fresh on every read, and it wobbles: "Kit I Notes(Kit", "Kit I Notes:Kiti" and "Kit I NotesiKit" are one fan. At rebuild, canonical_names() folds spellings together:

  1. Count reads per (account, spelling), and the most common Total spend seen in that spelling's panel reads.
  2. Walk spellings from most-read to least. Each one joins the first more-read spelling that is close, provided their spends don't contradict each other:
    • names of 4+ letters with a similarity of 0.75 or more (NAME_SIMILAR), or one containing the other (6+ letters);
    • or the same non-zero spend to the cent plus the same first 4 letters.
  3. Short names must match exactly: "TW" is not "TW MAX RUDY".

The spend check is what keeps two different fans called "Raul" apart when one has spent $364 and the other $12.34k. Every spelling read is listed in the chat file's names, and the viewer shows "N spellings".

09The fan panel

Panel reads go to the same journal ("kind": "panel"). Each fan's file keeps:

  • panel[tab]: the latest reading of each tab;
  • panel_log: every reading with its time, so spend changes over the shift are visible. The viewer shows them as "Spend during the shift".

On the first live shift: 312 panel snapshots, and 59 fans at $100+ total spend.

10Pictures you sent new today

A PPV's caption is half the story; its first image is the other half (what was teased, what he was buying). So every read also keeps a picture of each of your media bubbles, live and from video:

  1. After the turns are read, keep_media() looks at each of your text-less turns (media) that is fully on screen (not cut by the pane's top or bottom) and at least 60 px on each side.
  2. It crops that bubble from the same frame the turns came from. For a PPV that's the first image of the bundle, with Infloww's "1/6" counter on it.
  3. It computes a 16×16 average hash. If this fan's folder already has a picture within 24 of 256 bits (MEDIA_SAME), it's the same picture read again: the file is reused, and replaced only if the new crop is bigger. Otherwise it saves userdata/shiftlog/media/<account>/<fan>/<hash>.jpg (JPEG quality 88).
  4. The turn gets media_img (the path) before it's journaled. The link lives in the raw journal, so rebuilds keep it, and merging fills it into the chat's message.

The Shift Viewer shows the picture in place of the grey "photo / video" box. It asks for each picture once over its bridge (Api.media, path-checked, never the static server) and caches it.

  • Only your side. Pictures fans send aren't kept.
  • An image that is only ever seen cut off by the pane edge isn't kept. Scroll it fully into view once and it is.
  • Switch: Settings ▸ Features ▸ "Shift recorder: keep a picture of each image/PPV you send" (ShiftMedia). A failure to save a picture never costs the read.

Tested on frames from both recordings: whole bundle-cover images were cut out cleanly (600×600 in the enlarged crop); 7 sightings of the same pictures became 5 files; half-visible ones were correctly skipped.

11Video: recordings to chats

src/services/llm/video2chat.py runs the same reading and the same journal on a video file. The differences, and why:

Sampling

One frame every EVERY_S = 0.5 s of video. Decoding is the expensive part, so the frames in between are only grabbed (skipped cheaply). The settle rule is the same as live: pane, panel and header unchanged for SETTLE = 2 samples, then all read from one frame.

Lines by contrast, not colour

Scaled and compressed, the 1 px #444444 divider comes out around #2c2c2c, and once scaled down it's often 2 px of blur. So the video tool never looks for a colour; it looks for contrast. A pixel is "on a line" when it's at least LINE_CONTRAST = 3 grey levels brighter than the pixels on both sides, 1 px away or 2 px away (_line_map). Bubble colours survive compression (#353535 stays 53), so bubble finding is unchanged.

OCR engine

Video uses PP-OCRv5, English model (RapidOCR 3.10), swapped in for this process only. On compressed 1080p text the live engine dropped the spaces ("ialsousedmyvoice") and lost header names; v5 read both cleanly. It's installed with --no-deps plus colorlog and omegaconf to avoid an OpenCV clash.

Pop-ups

A scripts panel or similar pop-up over the composer draws its own horizontal line higher up. Each frame, if such a line crosses the pane, the pane is cut above it (frame_layout).

Time and account

  • Each read is stamped video start + offset. The start comes from an OBS-style file name (2026-04-03 00-52-29.mp4), or else from the file's modified time minus its length. Messages also carry their own footer times, so nothing depends on this being exact.
  • The account can't be read reliably from video, so pass --account "PINK VIP". Otherwise the file name is used.
  • A read with no fan name in the header (the chat list, a pop-up) is skipped: from video those were all junk.

Before: one rectangle per video

The tool used to measure Infloww's position once per video and apply it to every frame. A Discord frame was read as if it were Infloww, and only the "no fan name, skip" rule kept most of that junk out, by luck.

Now: every frame is located

locate(frame) finds Infloww's rectangle from its own geometry, or returns nothing. Precisely:

  1. Vertical lines. Compute the contrast line map. For each column, find its longest stretch that is a line, allowing breaks of up to RUN_GAP = 40 rows (a highlighted composer row or a pop-up crossing the divider), as long as it's a line on at least RUN_DENSE = 55% of that stretch. Text never passes this; its strokes line up for only a few rows. Keep columns whose stretch is ≥ MIN_RUN = 18% of the frame height, and keep one per cluster of neighbouring columns.
  2. Pairs. Every pair of such lines is a candidate (left divider, right divider). They must be at least 15% of the frame width apart and share most of their height.
  3. Header line. Between the pair, in the top 30% of their height (from 20 px above where they start), look at horizontal lines covering ≥ H_SHARE = 30% of the width. 30%, not more: shrunk to 0.69×, the 1 px line survives on only about a third of its width. Of the lines at least 70% as strong as the strongest, take the lowest (_header_line). The tab strip's line just above the header is just as strong; "strongest" picked either one, 35 px apart. A faint bubble edge lower down never reaches 70%.
  4. Scale from the left edge. The chat list is 400 px wide at 100%. Left of the left divider, look for the window's edge: a column where at least 70% of the rows step by 8+ grey levels (Discord's sidebar against Infloww, or a black letterbox against Infloww), taking the one nearest the list. No edge means Infloww starts at the frame's edge, as in full screen. scale = (left divider − edge) / 400, and it must be 0.3–1.7.
  5. Composer line. The composer is a fixed ~166 px tall (COMPOSER; measured 157 px at 0.94). So the composer's top line must sit at bottom − 166 × scale, ±28 × scale. A Discord channel or a webcam grid has no line there.
  6. Right panel fits. The fan panel is 281 px × scale wide; the rectangle must not run off the frame's right side.
  7. No line inside the pane. If another full-height line sits between the pair, the pair is wrong. That happens when Discord's sidebar edge is paired with Infloww's real divider.
  8. Infloww's own background. The median grey under the message pane and under the chat list must be 14–28. In every Infloww frame of both recordings, full or windowed, both come out at exactly 20 (#141414). Discord's sidebar and channels are 40+. This check alone removed the last false positives.
  9. A line that is another candidate's window edge is not a divider. That stops Infloww's bright 1 px window border from being taken for the left divider, which gave the wrong scale on the small stream tile.
  10. Of the surviving candidates, the largest wins.

Tested on 60 frames spread over both recordings: no false positives. The windowed frames are found with the right scale, 0.69–0.71, and every full-screen frame where nothing covers Infloww is found at 0.94.

Following it through the video: Tracker

The full search takes about 50 ms, so it doesn't run on every frame:

  1. If Infloww's rectangle is already known, a cheap check (still_there): is the background under both columns still grey 14–28, and is the left divider still a line on ≥ 50% of its height, at the same place? If so, same window; read it.
  2. Otherwise the full locate() runs. If it finds the same rectangle (within 4 px and 6% scale), carry on.
  3. Same window, part covered. If the new rectangle has the same dividers and scale but a different top or bottom, something is covering part of Infloww. While the mouse is over a stream, Discord shades the stream's bottom and draws its call controls there, which cuts the dividers and the composer line short; a pop-up can hide the header line. Covering only ever shrinks the box, so: a box inside the known one means covered, skip the frame. A box containing the known one means the known one was the covered version; take the bigger. Anything else (the window really moved) must hold for MOVED = 6 samples (3 s) to be believed.
  4. A new rectangle anywhere else must show up in CONFIRM = 2 samples in a row before it's trusted. A one-frame misfire, such as a fade or half a transition, is ignored.
  5. Nothing found: the frame is skipped.
  6. Infloww coming back after AWAY = 4 samples (2 s) away, or at a new position or size, starts a new stretch. The settle rule starts over, and the next chat read is marked resumed, which the gap rule treats like opening the chat fresh (§07).

13Video: reading at one size

The OCR was tuned on full-screen 1080p, where Infloww is at 0.94× and the frame was enlarged 1.5× before reading, an effective 1.41×. 2× was worse, because PP-OCRv5 shrinks very large crops back down. Now, for every frame:

  1. read_view() cuts out just Infloww (from its left edge to the right panel's edge, from 150 px × scale above the header line to the window bottom). Discord's pixels never reach the OCR.
  2. It enlarges the crop by TARGET / scale (1.41 / 0.94 = 1.5 full screen, 1.41 / 0.70 ≈ 2 windowed), capped at 3×. --upscale can force a fixed factor.
  3. The pop-up cut is measured on the native pixels (enlarging smears 1 px lines), then the layout is scaled to the enlarged crop.

Too small: below MIN_SCALE = 0.5 nothing is read; the timeline says "Infloww, too small to read". The cutoff comes from testing. A real windowed stream at 0.71 read cleanly: fan name, every message, times, the chatter, a $200 PPV marked paid. Frames shrunk to under 0.5 fell apart (names garbled, under half the text right). There was no real recording between 0.5 and 0.7 to test, so 0.5 is a cautious line.

The 17-minute recording, extracted end to end: 42 Infloww stretches (full screen and windowed, between Discord), 12.5 min read and 4.2 min skipped as not Infloww. 119 chat reads and 73 panel reads in 14.5 min; 31 reads marked resumed; 8 messages linked to 5 kept pictures. Fan names read correctly in the windowed stretches too. One fix came out of it: a "name" with fewer than 2 letters (for example "1 1", a half-covered header) is now skipped like an empty one.

14Shift Viewer and the clone

Shift Viewer (tools/shiftview)

A desktop window (pywebview, title "MMA Shift Viewer", never "Infloww Messages") that draws the collected chats as Infloww. Start it with tools/shiftview/ShiftViewer.vbs.

  • Accounts as the pills at the top. Filters: All / Warnings / PPVs / Paid. Sorting and search. Reloads every 4 s while a shift is being recorded.
  • Chats are drawn in order, oldest at the top: orange Fragment sections (scroll-backs not joined yet), then the chat, with blue not seen lines at every gap.
  • Hovering a message shows the footer exactly as read and when it was first seen.
  • The right panel shows the latest insights plus "Spend during the shift" and "Read as" (all spellings).
  • Rebuild replays the whole journal (§05).

Infloww clone (tools/inflowwclone)

One renderer, IC.render(root, model, hooks), used by the Shift Viewer (read-only) and TestFlow (live, typeable). It's measured from your live window, and keeps the pixel contract the readers rely on: #444444 only on the pane lines, #262626 / #353535 bubbles, the ⋮ menu 3×13 at (x0−12, y1−19), and the header line at y = 133 when framed. MMA's own readers read the clone exactly as they read Infloww, which is what makes TestFlow work as a test bench.

17What it cannot do

  • See what was never on screen. Chat history from before the shift, and messages that arrive and scroll away while you're in another chat. These show as gaps, honestly marked, never invented.
  • Tell two Infloww windows apart in a video. If a stream shows a different account, or someone else's Infloww, it's read as Infloww under the account you passed. The next step would be reading the account pill at top-left.
  • Read tiny windows. Under 0.5× it's flagged, not read.
  • Read under modals. While Vault or another full-window modal covers Infloww, nothing is read; the frame fails the background check.
  • Half-visible pictures. A PPV image only ever seen cut off by the pane edge isn't kept (§10).
  • Paid colour unconfirmed. The "paid" pill colour comes from old notes, not a live measurement; a screenshot of a paid PPV would settle it. Same for the Sales and Notes panel tabs.
